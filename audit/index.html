A 12-person company with a site that looks like a side project.

Blue Sky Solar has the best proof on the island: thousands of panels, a full crew, a warehouse and a professional photo shoot. The current site hides almost all of it. Here is what we found, and what the new version does instead.

The first screen says nothing but the name.

Visitors see “BLUE SKY SOLAR BONAIRE” over a YouTube video. No promise, no button, no proof. On a phone the name itself is cut off.

Now

  • Headline reads “BLUE SKY SOLAR BON”, cut off at the edge
  • Video plays from YouTube, with their logo in the corner
  • Only call to action is a small “Go Solar” in the menu
  • reCAPTCHA badge covers the content

New

  • Headline is the outcome: “Your own power plant. Built in two days.”
  • Free roof design and WhatsApp buttons on the first screen
  • 5.0 from 46 reviews right under the buttons
  • Real crew photo, no third party player

The size of the business is buried.

8,788 panels and 1,088 batteries is a serious number for Bonaire. Right now it sits halfway down the page in small type, under a heading that says “We love solar”. The team photo, with forklift, truck and the whole crew, is darkened behind a text box and followed by an empty blue band.

Now

Proof is decoration. The scale of the company has to be discovered.

New

The crew photo is the hero, full colour. The numbers sit right under it in large type. A full crew roster shows there are real people behind every install.

Clip-art icons instead of their own installs.

They paid for a professional shoot of their technical rooms, inverters and roofs. The product section shows orange clip-art instead. Orange is not even their colour: the sign, the truck and the shirts are navy and blue.

Now

Every system gets one generic line. No payback, no “who is this for”.

New

Each system has a real install photo, who it is for, and the facts buyers ask first: payback years, blackout proof, litres of hot water.

Their best sales copy is on a page nobody finds.

Ferry went from $350 a month to a few dollars. Andy’s 87 kWh a day home now pulls zero from the grid. Rob paid more than the other quotes and says it was worth it. These stories live on a “Customer experiences” page that the main menu does not link to.

Now

Reviews appear as a long Trustindex widget repeated at the bottom of every page.

New

The outcome is the headline (“$350 a month. Now a few dollars.”) with the customer quote small underneath. Three short review quotes, including the one about paying more for quality.
